    Topic 1: Limits of Sequences

    1. Theory: What is a Limit?

    Imagine you are walking towards a wall. With each step, you cover half the remaining distance. You get closer and closer, but theoretically, you never touch it. In math, the "wall" is the Limit.

    We say a sequence \(a_n\) converges to a limit \(L\) if the numbers get closer and closer to \(L\) as \(n\) goes to infinity (\(\infty\)).

    2. Solved Example

    Question: Find the limit of the sequence \(a_n = \frac{2n + 1}{n}\).

    Solution: We divide every term by the highest power of \(n\) (which is \(n\)). $\(\frac{2n}{n} + \frac{1}{n} = 2 + \frac{1}{n}\)$ As \(n \to \infty\), the term \(\frac{1}{n}\) becomes \(0\). Result: \(2 + 0 = 2\).

    Topic 2: Limits of Functions (The "Hole")

    1. Theory: Value vs. Limit

    The limit asks "Where is the function going?", not "Where is the function at?". Sometimes a function has a "hole" at a specific point (like \(0/0\)), but we can still see where the graph approaches.

    2. Solved Example

    Question: Find \(\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}\).

    Solution: If we plug in \(x=3\), we get \(0/0\). So we must simplify using Difference of Squares: $\(x^2 - 9 = (x-3)(x+3)\)$ $\(\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)} = x+3\)$ Now plug in \(x=3\): \(3+3=6\).

    3. Your Turn (Quiz)

    Compute the limit of \(f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}\) as \(x \to 1\).

    Your Answer (Press Enter):

    Show Solution

    Factorize and simplify: $\(\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (x+1) = 2\)$

    Topic 3: Indeterminate Forms (\(0/0\))

    1. Theory: Algebra of Limits

    When you see \(\frac{0}{0}\), it does NOT mean "undefined" or "zero". It is an Indeterminate Form. It means "We need to do more work" (usually factoring) to find the hidden answer.

    2. Solved Example

    Question: Find \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x^2 + 10x}{x}\).

    Solution: Plug in 0: \(0/0\). Factor out \(x\) from the top: $\(\frac{x(5x + 10)}{x} = 5x + 10\)$ Now plug in \(x=0\): \(5(0) + 10 = 10\).

    Finale: Final Test

    Task A: Sequence Limit

    Find limit of \(a_n = \frac{3n}{n+2}\).

    Show Solution

    Divide by highest power (\(n\)): $\(\frac{3}{1 + 0} = 3\)$

    Task B: Function Limit

    Find limit of \(f(x) = \frac{x^2 + 3x}{x}\) as \(x \to 0\).

    Show Solution

    Factor out \(x\): $\(\frac{x(x+3)}{x} = x+3 \rightarrow 3\)$
