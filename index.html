    📘 Module 1: Limits of Sequences

    🧠 The Concept: Approaching the "Wall"

    Imagine walking halfway to a wall, then halfway again. You get closer effectively forever, but mathematically, do you touch it? This is the definition of a Limit (\(L\)).

    \[\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L\]

    👁️‍🗨️ Example: The Infinite Division

    Goal: Find the limit of \(a_n = \frac{2n + 1}{n}\) as \(n \to \infty\).

    Solution: Divide everything by \(n\): $\(\frac{2n}{n} + \frac{1}{n} = 2 + \frac{1}{n}\)$ Since \(\frac{1}{\infty} \to 0\), the result is 2.

    📗 Module 2: The "Hole" in the Graph

    🧠 The Concept: Limits vs. Values

    A function can be undefined at a point (like \(0/0\)) but still have a limit. We are looking for the "path", not the destination.

    👁️‍🗨️ Example: Removing the Glitch

    Goal: \(\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}\).

    Solution: Factor the top (Difference of squares): $\(\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)} = x+3\)$ Now it's safe to plug in \(x=3\). Result is 6.

    🚀 Challenge 2: Fix the Function

    Find limit of \(f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}\) as \(x \to 1\).

    Limit ($x \to 1$):

    💡 Stuck? Reveal Solution

    Factorize: \((x-1)(x+1)\). Cancel \((x-1)\). Result: \(1+1=2\).

    📙 Module 3: Indeterminate Forms

    🧠 The Concept: The 0/0 Mystery

    \(\frac{0}{0}\) is not zero. It means "Do more work". Usually, this means factoring or expanding terms.

    👁️‍🗨️ Example: Factoring Out \(x\)

    Goal: \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x^2 + 10x}{x}\).

    Solution: Factor out \(x\) from the numerator: \(x(5x+10)\). Cancel \(x\). Limit is \(5(0)+10 = 10\).

    🚀 Challenge 3: Algebra Practice

    Limit of \(f(x) = \frac{2x^2 - 2}{x - 1}\) as \(x \to 1\).

    Limit ($x \to 1$):

    💡 Stuck? Reveal Solution
    \[2(x^2-1) \to 2(x-1)(x+1) \to 4\]
